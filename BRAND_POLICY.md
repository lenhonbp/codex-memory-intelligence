## Modified forks and redistributed versions

If you distribute or operate a modified version, please make its independent status clear. In particular:

1. Do not present a modified fork as the original or official CMI project.
2. Do not imply endorsement, sponsorship, certification, or maintenance by the original project unless that relationship actually exists.
3. Use a distinct product or service name for a materially modified commercial distribution or hosted offering.
4. Clearly disclose that the version is modified or independently maintained when a reasonable user could otherwise confuse it with the original project.
5. Preserve notices required by the applicable software license.

## Names, logos, and visual identity

The software license does not itself grant trademark or branding rights. Nothing in that license should be read as permission to use the project name, abbreviation, logos, or visual identity in a misleading way or to imply that an independent product is official.

Truthful nominative or referential use is not the target of this policy. The goal is to avoid confusion about source, authorship, endorsement, and official status.

## Commercial products and services

The repository's current software license determines what commercial uses of the code are permitted. This brand policy does not add code-use restrictions that conflict with that license.

However, a commercial product, package, hosted service, or distribution built from a fork should not use the original project's identity in a way that makes users believe it is the original CMI project or an officially endorsed offering.

## Reporting confusing or misleading use

If you find a fork, package, service, or distribution that appears to remove required attribution, misrepresent authorship, or falsely present itself as the official project, please open an issue in the original repository with links and supporting evidence.
